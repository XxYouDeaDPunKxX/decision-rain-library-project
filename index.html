The real failure mode

Bookmarks remember the URL. They forget the reason.

You do not always know whether a link is useful when you save it. The value is often conditional: it might fit later, reveal a reusable pattern, require a quick spike, or deserve the archive.

You drop a repo into chat.It has one sharp idea, but setup friction may make it a no.
You find a tool at the wrong time.Worth preserving, not worth adopting today.
You save a long guide.You need the useful claim and next step, not another abandoned tab.
You collect a directory.Many candidates, no decision trail, high drift risk.

What keeps it sane

Boundaries that stop link capture from becoming false certainty.

The repository stays small on purpose: docs, templates, examples, and authority boundaries that make AI-assisted link review repeatable without giving the assistant final say.

Inbox-first capture

Every incoming signal starts preserved before classification. Capture does not pretend to be validation.

Separated judgment fields

Claim, evidence, authority, confidence, practical fit, risk, verdict, and next action stay distinct.

Human approval gate

The assistant may inspect and propose. Promotion to library, archive, taxonomy changes, and canonical decisions require operator approval.

Controlled tag grammar

Tags describe source, type, domain, authority, confidence state, status, risk, fit, scenario, priority, and next action.

Private corpus boundary

The public repo carries the skeleton. Your real saved links, notes, decisions, and operator history stay inside your Raindrop setup.

Small useful surface

Not a full PKM, not a crawler platform, not silent automation. It is a decision trail for links that may matter later.

Daily use

Scope control

Useful because it refuses to become everything.

Not a complete PKM.

It does not try to organize your whole knowledge life. It solves the saved-link decision gap.

Not an automation framework.

It can work with connectors, but the core safety property is human approval, not silent writes.

Not a Raindrop clone.

Raindrop remains the backend. This project adds the decision grammar around saved links.
